## Evidence Ladder and How to Read This Report

This survey is the second rung of four kinds of evidence. It reports what surveyed completers say; it does not show that training caused any change.
- ### Randomized Trial (BIGD)

+10.3 pp

more employment, in percentage points, for women offered a place (randomized trial of a predecessor program)

What it shows

The effect of one CodersTrust program (WSDFM) on women in Dhaka, against a randomly chosen control group.

What it does not show

Results for other countries, groups or today’s standard track.
- ### Impact Survey 2026 (Surveyed Completers)

77.9%

employed, up from 47.4%, among surveyed completers (October 2026)

What it shows

What alumni who responded report about work and income after training.

What it does not show

Cause: respondents are not a random sample and may over-represent successful learners.
- ### Program Results

711 of 1,000

women in work (71%) in WSDFM

What it shows

How many completers of a program were in work, from CodersTrust program records.

What it does not show

What would have happened without training.
- ### Learner Stories

Individual learner stories

What it shows

What the path can look like for one person.

What it does not show

A typical or promised outcome; some are outliers and say so.

## Who Responded

Respondents were predominantly degree holders; some were employed before training (47.4% were already working). Respondent profile: 84.6% hold a bachelor’s degree or higher; 68.7% aged 15–35; 19.9% women; 62.9% live outside Dhaka; 58 of 64 districts; 16.0% rural.

## Speed to Earnings

Among the 170 surveyed completers who first earned during or after training: 23.5% first earned during the course; 58.8% within six months; 77.1% within a year; 90.6% within two years (cumulative).

## Methodology & Limitations
- Survey. CodersTrust Impact Survey, October 2026: an online alumni survey (LimeSurvey survey 112268), fielded in October 2026.
- Who the respondents are. 84.6% of respondents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, so results describe a mostly graduate group.
