## The Short Answer

A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed. Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher. Employment rose 10.3 percentage points for everyone offered a place (13.6 for attenders), on a control-group rate of 52.8%.

For every 100 women offered a place, about 10 more were in work at follow-up than among 100 comparable women in the control group (+10.3 percentage points).

## Evidence-and-Fit Brief

What was evaluated

The WSDFM program, a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track.

Who took part

Women aged 18–35 with a Secondary School Certificate (SSC) or above, in Dhaka.

Offered-place effects first

Employment rose 10.3 percentage points for women offered a place, on a control-group rate of 52.8%; effects for women who attended come second.

What the 2026 survey adds

Descriptive outcomes among surveyed completers across 58 of 64 districts.

What a new pilot must establish

Country, priority groups and the 100-hour track, agreed in co-design.

## How the Trial Worked

Trial design: 833 women at baseline (425 treatment, 408 control) and 599 surveyed at follow-up; the predecessor program was 200 hours over 4 months, plus 3 months of mentoring.

## What the Trial Found

### Women Offered a Place

- Employment rose 10.3 percentage points for everyone offered a place (13.6 for attenders), on a control-group rate of 52.8%.
- A randomized controlled trial of CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program found that women offered a place were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed (a 20% relative increase). Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher. For women who attended, income was 54% higher and employment 26% higher than the control group.
- About +$27 a month for everyone offered a place (+$36 for attenders) on a control-group mean of about $66.

### Women Who Attended

- For attenders, freelancing employment was 184% higher than the control group’s, freelance hours 168% higher and freelance income 169% higher (about 2.8×, 2.7× and 2.7×); overall hours worked were 42% higher. For everyone offered a place, freelance income was 2.3× the control group’s.
- Household effects for women who attended, against the control group: food spending +48% and non-food spending +41%, share with savings +41%, fewer took a loan, and “never felt stressed in the last month” +83%.

## What It Means for Funders

The offered-place results are the closer guide to what a funded program can deliver, because they include women who were offered a place but did not attend. The attender results show what training did for the women who took part.

One trial in one population does not forecast a new country or a new track. That is why a new pilot starts from a scorecard agreed in co-design, with independent evaluation welcome. How Partnerships Work

## Context: How This Compares

Context (external): A review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

What the trial taught us about advising: Only 39% of WSDFM trainees attended at least one advising session; 7% attended more than five, and BIGD found training worked best when complemented by advising — which is why CodersTrust mentoring is milestone-based, with attendance tracked and follow-up on non-attendance.

## Questions About the Evidence

### Is There Independent Evidence?

Yes — a randomized controlled trial by BIGD (BRAC University) of CodersTrust’s WSDFM program studied women in Dhaka trained in 2020, on a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track. Women offered a place were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed (52.8% of the control group was employed), and their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher. For women who attended, income was 54% higher and employment 26% higher. The trial surveyed 599 women at follow-up (report published May 2022). For context, a review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

### How Do You Prove Outcomes?

Baseline at enrollment, tracer surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months, and documentary evidence of earnings where available; independent evaluation is welcome. Evidence includes payslips and offer letters for jobs, platform logs or bank receipts for freelancing; requirements are agreed in co-design. Funders receive monthly reports on enrollment, completion, certification, placements and first earnings.
