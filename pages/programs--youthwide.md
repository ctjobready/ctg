Decision Summary

## YouthWIDE at a Glance

Where It Runs

Co-funded country cohorts, designed for South Asia, the Middle East and North Africa, and Sub-Saharan Africa. Bangladesh is the reference implementation and has a national initiative of its own.

Who It Is For

Governments, development partners and funders who want to report on educated, unemployed young people moving into paid digital work.

Delivery

Blended training in existing labs and colleges, with certified local facilitators; a central team coordinating local labs for larger programs.

Training and Mentoring

100 training hours per certification with the exam fee included, then three months of milestone-based mentoring.

Placement Support

Placement support into local, remote and global work.

Reporting

Monthly reports, follow-up surveys and documentary evidence of earnings where available, as agreed in the program scope.

The Cost of Waiting

## Training Without Work Entry Spends the Budget Without the Result

### A Review of Nine Trials Found Only Three with Significant Employment Impacts

A review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

What It Means

Too many funders receive attendance and completion counts but little evidence of earnings.

### On One Major Freelancing Platform, Automation-Prone Postings Fell After ChatGPT’s Release

Within eight months of ChatGPT’s release, postings for automation-prone writing and coding jobs on a major freelancing platform fell 21% relative to manual-intensive jobs, and image-creation postings fell 17% within about a year of image-generating AI.

What It Means

Training has to follow current employer demand, not last year’s job market.

It doesn’t have to be this way.

## Questions Funders and Agencies Ask

### Is YouthWIDE Right for Our Organization?

Right for you if you want employment outcomes tracked after blended training delivered through existing labs and colleges. YouthWIDE is not the right fit if you need a 100,000+ rollout delivered by one provider in a single phase; we plan national delivery phase by phase.

### Will This Work in Our Country?

That depends on local demand, which we test first. Our outcome evidence comes mainly from Bangladesh, and the Kosovo pilot adds program results from outside it. CodersTrust has run programs since 2014 in 15 countries and territories.

### Can You Deliver at National Scale?

Her Power, a national program of DoICT, the ICT Division, trained 25,125 women across 130 sub-districts (upazilas). CodersTrust provided the curriculum, training manuals and mentorship guidelines and trained 2,500 women directly.

We scale in phases, up to 10 cohorts in parallel, with certified local facilitators. A 100,000+ phase is a design option; we have not yet delivered a program at that size.

### How Do You Prove Outcomes?

As agreed in the program scope, we plan a baseline at enrollment, follow-up surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months and monthly reports for every cohort, with documentary evidence of earnings where available. Independent evaluation is welcome.

### How Fast Can We Start?

For grant-funded pilots, agreement typically takes about three weeks from first conversation, and the first cohort is typically in training 6–8 weeks after signing. Counting from the first conversation, Week 1 is a discovery session, Week 2 co-design and Week 3 the agreement; public procurement follows the agency’s own timeline.
