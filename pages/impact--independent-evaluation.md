# Randomized Trial: Women Offered a Place Were More Likely to Work

In a randomized trial by the BRAC Institute of Governance and Development (BIGD), women offered a place were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed than the control group. The trial evaluated CodersTrust’s 200-hour WSDFM program for women in Dhaka, not today’s 100-hour tracks.

## The Result

Randomized Trial

CodersTrust’s WSDFM program, women in Dhaka

For every 100 women offered a place, about 10 more were in work at follow-up than among 100 comparable women in the control group (+10.3 percentage points).

Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher than the control group’s (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher than the control group’s.

### How to Read This Trial
- What was evaluated. CodersTrust’s 200-hour WSDFM program; today’s tracks are 100 hours.
- Who took part. Women in Dhaka with a Secondary School Certificate (SSC) or above, most of them aged 18–35.
- Design. 833 women at baseline (425 treatment, 408 control) and 599 surveyed at follow-up; CodersTrust’s WSDFM program was 200 hours over 4 months, plus 3 months of mentoring.

## Income, Freelancing and Household Effects

### Women Offered a Place
- About +$27 a month for all women offered a place, on a control-group mean of about $66.

### Women Who Attended
- Income about +$36 a month.
- Employment 26% higher (13.6 percentage points more); freelancing 2.8× the control group.
- Household effects for women who attended, against the control group: higher food and non-food spending, more of them with savings, fewer loans and less stress.

The report gives these amounts in taka: BDT 2,354 a month for women offered a place, BDT 3,118 for attenders, on a control-group mean of BDT 5,715 (Table 4); the dollar figures are approximate conversions at about BDT 86 to the dollar, the rate in November–December 2021, when the follow-up survey ran.

## Over Four Times the Average of Nine Training Trials

Employment effect over four times the average across nine training trials: +10.3 percentage points for women offered a place, against an average of 2.3 points across nine randomized trials of vocational training reviewed by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017), only three of which found a significant employment effect.

## Published in a Peer-Reviewed Journal

The trial is also published in a peer-reviewed journal: Arman, Bhattacharjee, Das, Gomes and Mahmood (2026), “Tackling Women’s Unemployment: Evidence from an Online Jobs Experiment in Bangladesh,” Economic Development and Cultural Change 75(1): 367–399 (October 2026). https://doi.org/10.1086/741450

(opens doi.org)

The journal article estimates a benefit/cost ratio of 3.01 for CodersTrust’s 200-hour WSDFM program.

The results above come from BIGD’s report. The article is a separate publication.

## What It Means for Funders

The offered-place results are the closer guide to what a funded program can deliver, because they include women who were offered a place but did not attend. One trial in one population does not forecast a new country or a new track, so a new pilot starts from a scorecard agreed in co-design, with independent evaluation welcome.

BIGD’s long-term follow-up study of the program is under way. See BIGD’s Follow-Up Study

## How We Track Outcomes

For every cohort, as agreed in the program scope, we plan a baseline at enrollment, follow-up surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months and monthly reports. Where available, offer letters, payslips, platform logs or bank receipts document work and earnings.
