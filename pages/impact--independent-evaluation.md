# A Randomized Trial of CodersTrust’s Predecessor Program for Women in Dhaka

A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program, a 200-hour program for women in Dhaka, were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed than the control group. It is evidence about that predecessor program, not a measurement of today’s 100-hour tracks.

## The Short Answer

Randomized Trial

Predecessor WSDFM program, women in Dhaka

For every 100 women offered a place, about 10 more were in work at follow-up than among 100 comparable women in the control group (+10.3 percentage points).

Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher than the control group’s (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher than the control group’s.

## Evidence-and-Fit Brief

What was evaluated

The WSDFM program, a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track.

Who took part

Women aged 18–35 with a Secondary School Certificate (SSC) or above, in Dhaka.

Offered-place effects first

Results for women offered a place come first, then the results for women who attended.

What the 2026 survey adds

Descriptive outcomes among surveyed completers across 58 of 64 districts.

What a new pilot must establish

Country, priority groups and the 100-hour track, agreed in co-design.

## How the Trial Worked

Trial design: 833 women at baseline (425 treatment, 408 control) and 599 surveyed at follow-up; the predecessor program was 200 hours over 4 months, plus 3 months of mentoring.

## What the Trial Found

The headline results are above. This section adds what the trial found beyond them.

### Women Offered a Place
- About +$27 a month for all women offered a place (+$36 for attenders) on a control-group mean of about $66.
- WSDFM employment status at follow-up (BIGD): control group 47.2% unemployed, 40.9% other employment, 11.9% freelancing; women offered a place 36.9% unemployed, 34.6% other employment, 28.5% freelancing.

### Women Who Attended
- Employment 26% higher (13.6 percentage points more); freelancing 2.8× the control group.
- For attenders, freelancing employment was 184% higher than the control group’s, freelance hours 168% higher and freelance income 169% higher (about 2.8×, 2.7× and 2.7×); overall hours worked were 42% higher. For all women offered a place, freelance income was 2.3× the control group’s.
- Household effects for women who attended, against the control group: food spending +48% and non-food spending +41%, share with savings +41%, fewer took a loan, and “never felt stressed in the last month” +83%.

## How This Compares with Other Training Trials

For context, here is how the employment effect for women offered a place compares with other randomized trials of vocational training.

Employment effect over four times the average across nine training trials: +10.3 percentage points for women offered a place, against an average of 2.3 points across nine randomized trials of vocational training reviewed by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017), only three of which found a significant employment effect.

What the trial taught us about advising: In the predecessor WSDFM program, 39% of trainees attended at least one advising session; CodersTrust mentoring is milestone-based so attendance can be tracked.

## What It Means for Funders

The offered-place results are the closer guide to what a funded program can deliver, because they include women who were offered a place but did not attend. The study also reports results for women who attended.

One trial in one population does not forecast a new country or a new track. That is why a new pilot starts from a scorecard agreed in co-design, with independent evaluation welcome.

BIGD has a five-year follow-up study planned. See How Partnerships Work

## Questions About the Evidence

### Is There Independent Evidence?

Yes — a randomized controlled trial by BIGD (BRAC University) of CodersTrust’s WSDFM program studied women in Dhaka trained in 2020, on a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track. It found that women offered a place were more likely to be employed than the control group. The trial surveyed 599 women at follow-up (report published May 2022). For context, a review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

### How Do You Track Outcomes?

For every cohort, as agreed in the program scope, we plan a baseline at enrollment, tracer surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months and monthly reports. Documentary evidence of earnings is collected where available: offer letters document job offers; payslips document employment earnings; platform logs or bank receipts document freelance earnings. Monthly reports cover enrollment, completion, certification, placements and first earnings. Independent evaluation is welcome.
