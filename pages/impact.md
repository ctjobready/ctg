# Impact and Evidence

What CodersTrust results show, what kind of evidence each one is, and what it does not show.

[image: A group of women in matching purple at a Her Power program event]

In Short

A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed. Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher.

Among surveyed completers, employment rose from 47.4% before training to 77.9% at the October 2026 survey.

Results at a Glance

## The Evidence Ladder: Four Kinds of Evidence

Strongest for cause first. Each rung says what it shows, what it does not show and how it was made.

- 1 Randomized Trial (BIGD) +10.3 pp more employment, in percentage points, for women offered a place (randomized trial of a predecessor program) What it shows The effect of one CodersTrust program (WSDFM) on women in Dhaka, against a randomly chosen control group. What it does not show Results for other countries, groups or today’s standard track. The Randomized Trial
- 2 Impact Survey 2026 (Surveyed Completers) 77.9% employed, up from 47.4%, among surveyed completers (October 2026) What it shows What alumni who responded report about work and income after training. What it does not show Cause: respondents are not a random sample and may over-represent successful learners. Outcomes 2026
- 3 Program Results 711 of 1,000 women in work (71%) in WSDFM What it shows How many completers of a program were in work, from CodersTrust program records. What it does not show What would have happened without training. Case Studies
- 4 Learner Stories Individual stories, in learners’ own words What it shows What the path can look like for one person. What it does not show A typical or promised outcome; some are outliers and say so. Success Stories

## Contribution to the Sustainable Development Goals

CodersTrust’s training-to-employment model contributes to seven SDGs — core: quality education (4), gender equality (5), decent work and economic growth (8); also: no poverty (1), zero hunger (2), industry and innovation (9), reduced inequalities (10).
