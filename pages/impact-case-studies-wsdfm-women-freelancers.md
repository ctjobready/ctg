Context

## Why This Program

Women in Bangladesh often lack the skills and training to succeed in the workforce, which limits their employment opportunities.

Porticus commissioned CodersTrust to run a skills project for women, focused on ICT skills for freelancing marketplaces and local jobs, and on women’s empowerment.

[image: Women trainees and a CodersTrust Bangladesh banner at a WSDFM training session]

A WSDFM training session.

## Challenges

- Low awareness of freelancing made students uninterested.
- Families did not always support their studies.
- Many trainees lacked basic computer literacy, a computer or an internet connection.
- Social obstacles drove dropout up.
- COVID-19 closed physical classrooms.

## What CodersTrust Did

- Campus activation, roadshows, seminars, parent counseling sessions and online campaigns.
- Basic computer literacy added to the curriculum.
- Family counseling and a stipend for active students.
- Online training sessions during COVID-19.
- One-to-one mentoring support for students.

Results

## What Happened

Program Results

WSDFM (Women’s Skill Development for Freelancing Marketplace), commissioned by Porticus: 711 of 1,000 women in work (71%); independently evaluated by BIGD (randomized controlled trial).

### What the Randomized Trial Found

BIGD randomized trial, May 2022

A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed. Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher.

Employment Status at Follow-Up: Women Offered a Place vs the Control Group

- Control Group
  - Unemployed: 47.2%
  - Other Employment: 40.9%
  - Freelancing: 11.9%
- Women Offered a Place
  - Unemployed: 36.9%
  - Other Employment: 34.6%
  - Freelancing: 28.5%

BIGD randomized trial; women offered a place vs control group (intent to treat). Follow-up November–December 2021.

### Women Who Attended

BIGD randomized trial, May 2022

For attenders, freelancing employment was 184% higher than the control group’s, freelance hours 168% higher and freelance income 169% higher (about 2.8×, 2.7× and 2.7×); overall hours worked were 42% higher. For everyone offered a place, freelance income was 2.3× the control group’s.
