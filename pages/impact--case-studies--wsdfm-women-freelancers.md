Context

## Why This Program

Porticus commissioned CodersTrust to run a skills project for women, focused on ICT skills for freelancing marketplaces and local jobs, and on women’s empowerment.

Women in Bangladesh have limited access to the skills and training needed to succeed in the workforce, which restricts their employment opportunities.

[image: Women trainees and a CodersTrust Bangladesh banner at a WSDFM training session]

A WSDFM training session.

## Challenges
- Low awareness of freelancing reduced initial interest.
- Many trainees lacked basic computer literacy, a computer or an internet connection.
- Social obstacles drove dropout up.

## What CodersTrust Did
- Campus outreach, roadshows, seminars, parent counseling sessions and online campaigns.
- Basic computer literacy added to the curriculum.
- Family counseling and a stipend for active students.
- One-to-one mentoring support for students.

Results

## What Happened

Program Results

Program Record

WSDFM (Women’s Skill Development for Freelancing Marketplace), commissioned by Porticus: program records report that 711 of the 1,000 women enrolled were in work (71%); BIGD evaluated the predecessor WSDFM program in a randomized trial.

### What the Randomized Trial Found

Randomized Trial

BIGD randomized trial, May 2022

A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed. Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher than the control group’s (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher than the control group’s.

Employment Status at Follow-Up: Women Offered a Place vs. the Control Group
- Control Group
- Unemployed: 47.2%
- Other Employment: 40.9%
- Freelancing: 11.9%
- Women Offered a Place
- Unemployed: 36.9%
- Other Employment: 34.6%
- Freelancing: 28.5%

BIGD randomized trial; women offered a place vs. control group (intent to treat). Follow-up November–December 2021.
