## What Is CodersTrust?

CodersTrust is a workforce-development organization that trains and mentors educated, unemployed and underemployed youth in emerging markets for digital work and connects them to local, remote and global work.

CodersTrust is building JobReady, an AI-native digital education and employment platform for emerging markets, on a track record of 130,000+ learners trained since 2014.

The Thesis

## Skills and Work Are Not Meeting.

Educated young people are not in work or training, and employers cannot find skills. Courses that sell content do not close the gap, and AI is raising the bar for entry work.

### Online Learning Sells Content, Not Earnings.

Many online courses provide content without the work-entry support that turns skills into income: mentoring, certification and placement.

### AI Is Raising the Bar for Entry Work.

Globally, jobs requiring AI skills grew 69% vs 9% for all jobs, with a 62% wage premium

Within eight months of ChatGPT’s release, postings for automation-prone writing and coding jobs on a major freelancing platform fell 21% relative to manual-intensive jobs, and image-creation postings fell 17% within about a year of image-generating AI.

Every cohort that is trained but not employed leaves that gap open.

It doesn’t have to be this way.

Where We Stand

For governments, development partners, foundations and employers who need youth moved into paid digital work, with results that are measured, reported and open to independent evaluation, CodersTrust offers an integrated training-to-earnings model as country programs. Unlike approaches that typically focus on one part of the path — training-only programs, self-paced course platforms or freelance marketplaces alone (provision varies by provider) — we design, deliver and track the whole path to earnings, working in phases through existing labs and colleges, with outcome evidence that includes a randomized trial by BIGD.

### Is the JobReady Platform Live?

Partly — modules are rolling out in stages. Live today: partner portals, marketing automation, CRM and AI chat. In pre-launch: the employer and gig-work (Earn) marketplace, so placement also runs through CodersTrust’s placement team and marketplaces such as Upwork and Fiverr.

### Will AI Eliminate the Jobs CodersTrust Trains For?

Some entry-level tasks in our tracks are exposed, so we choose tracks from current employer demand, set per country. Within eight months of ChatGPT’s release, postings for automation-prone writing and coding work on a major freelancing platform fell 21% relative to manual-intensive work, while the World Economic Forum expects AI and information processing to create 11 million jobs and displace 9 million worldwide by 2030. AI-ready curriculum: each track’s AI content and work-entry plan are agreed with you before enrollment; we do not yet publish a track-by-track breakdown.

### Can You Deliver at National Scale?

We scale in phases, starting from what we have done: government contracts with training scopes ranging from 120+ professionals to 3,120+ digital-lab staff, and the curriculum for a national program that trained 25,125 women (with 2,500 women trained directly); separately, 10,000 teachers were trained to teach online during COVID-19. Larger programs run hub-and-spoke — up to 10 cohorts in parallel, certified local facilitators and blended delivery in existing labs and colleges; in Bangladesh, 9,000+ government digital labs could host blended learning; lab access and seats are confirmed per program. A 100,000+ national program is a design option we plan phase by phase; we have not yet delivered one at that size.

### Is There Independent Evidence?

Yes — a randomized controlled trial by BIGD (BRAC University) of CodersTrust’s WSDFM program studied women in Dhaka trained in 2020, on a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track. Women offered a place were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed (52.8% of the control group was employed), and their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher. For women who attended, income was 54% higher and employment 26% higher. The trial surveyed 599 women at follow-up (report published May 2022). For context, a review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.
