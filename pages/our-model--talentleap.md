# What Is TalentLEAP? CodersTrust’s Competency-Based Talent Development Model

TalentLEAP is CodersTrust’s competency-based talent development model. It breaks each career path into individual skill units aligned with industry expectations, teaches and assesses them in graduated competency levels (after the Dreyfus model of skill acquisition), and carries every learner through three stages — Learn, Earn and Prosper — with three months of milestone-based mentoring after the course.

## At a Glance
- Among surveyed completers, employment rose from 47.4% before training to 77.9% at the October 2026 survey (a +30.5 percentage-point change).
- Among the 170 surveyed completers who first earned during or after training: 23.5% first earned during the course; 58.8% within six months; 77.1% within a year; 90.6% within two years (cumulative). This is a timing measure only.
- A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed. Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher.

These results describe CodersTrust programs, not TalentLEAP’s features on their own: neither the survey nor the trial isolates one part of the model.

Assess, Learn and Prepare for Certification make up the Learn stage; Earn and Prosper follow.
- ### Step 1: Assess and Place on a Track

An accessible readiness assessment (tests and an interview) routes each applicant to a career track or to a foundation track with extra support. A small number may not be selected, and the reasons are reported.
- ### Step 2: Learn Skill Units

Each track teaches individual skill units, assessed against common rubrics: 100 training hours per certification, half of them hands-on labs.
- ### Step 3: Certify

Certification exam preparation (exam fee included).
- ### Step 4: Earn

Three months of milestone-based one-to-one mentoring, with placement support alongside, toward each track’s agreed work-entry goal (for freelance tracks: profile live, first bids, first client).
- ### Step 5: Prosper

Career paths with progressive levels, continuing professional development and the alumni network.

Certifications run by track, for example Meta Blueprint and Google Ads for digital marketing, Adobe Certified Professional for design and video, QuickBooks Online ProAdvisor for digital accounting, PCEP for Python and CWP for web.

CodersTrust is not affiliated with or endorsed by these certifying bodies; it prepares learners for their exams.

## What Makes TalentLEAP Different
- Competency-based rather than time-based. Learning is organized around individual skill units that are assessed, not around hours attended alone.
- Milestone-based mentoring. Mentoring follows milestones toward each track’s agreed work-entry goal, with placement support alongside, and attendance is tracked.
- Demand-led tracks. Tracks are set from current employer demand in each country, and each track’s work-entry plan is approved before enrollment.

Design Rationale, Not a Proof of Mentoring

In the predecessor WSDFM program, 39% of trainees attended at least one advising session; CodersTrust mentoring is milestone-based so attendance can be tracked.

The randomized trial’s program included three months of mentoring, but the trial did not isolate the effect of mentoring, so this page makes no claim that mentoring itself is proven.

Context (external): A review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

## Questions About TalentLEAP

### How Is CodersTrust Different from an Online Course Platform?

Self-paced course platforms focus on content access; our programs add mentoring, certification exam preparation and placement support, agreed in each program’s scope. Every TalentLEAP cohort pairs 100 training hours per certification with three months of milestone-based mentoring, certification exam preparation (exam fee included), placement support and outcome tracking for 12 months.

### What Happens When Funding Ends?

Where included in the program scope, what the program builds stays in place: certified local facilitators and localized curricula, and completers join CodersTrust’s alumni network after their three months of mentoring. TalentLEAP is designed to organize skills into career paths with progressive levels, so completers can keep advancing after a funded cohort ends.

### How Do You Prove Outcomes?

Baseline at enrollment, tracer surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months, and documentary evidence of earnings where available; independent evaluation is welcome. Offer letters document job offers; payslips document employment earnings; platform logs or bank receipts document freelance earnings where available. Funders receive monthly reports on enrollment, completion, certification, placements and first earnings.

### Is There Independent Evidence?

Yes — a randomized controlled trial by BIGD (BRAC University) of CodersTrust’s WSDFM program studied women in Dhaka trained in 2020, on a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track. It found that women offered a place were more likely to be employed than the control group. The trial surveyed 599 women at follow-up (report published May 2022). For context, a review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

### Do Your Learners Only Freelance?

No — salaried jobs remain the largest pathway; freelancing and business ownership grew the most in relative terms. Among surveyed completers, salaried employment rose from 38.3% before training to 53.3% at the October 2026 survey, freelancing from 6.2% to 14.0% and business ownership from 2.8% to 10.6%.

### Who Are Your Typical Learners?

Mostly university graduates from across Bangladesh, some of them already working before training. Among surveyed completers, 84.6% hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, 68.7% were aged 15–35 and 62.9% live outside Dhaka, across 58 of the country’s 64 districts; 47.4% were working before training.
