## An Integrated Path from Training to Earnings

Our Approach

For development partners and NGOs who need youth employment results they can report, CodersTrust offers an integrated training-to-earnings model with outcome tracking after each course, as agreed in the program scope. Unlike approaches that typically focus on one part of the path — training-only programs, self-paced course platforms or freelance marketplaces alone (provision varies by provider) — we design, deliver and track the path from training toward paid work, working in phases through existing labs and colleges, with outcome evidence that includes a randomized trial by BIGD.

YouthWIDE is the program through which funders deploy the CodersTrust model in a country. It is designed for deployment in other countries; current outcome evidence comes from Bangladesh and Kosovo.

## An Independent Randomized Trial

For every 100 women offered a place, about 10 more were in work at follow-up than among 100 comparable women in the control group (+10.3 percentage points).

A randomized trial by BIGD found that women offered a place in CodersTrust’s predecessor WSDFM program were 10.3 percentage points more likely to be employed. Their monthly income was an estimated 41% higher than the control group’s (about +$27 a month). For women who attended, income was 54% higher than the control group’s.

## Questions Partners Ask

### Will This Work in Our Country?

That depends on local demand in your portfolio countries, which we test first. Our outcome evidence comes mainly from Bangladesh, and the Kosovo pilot adds program results from outside it. We set each country’s tracks with local partners, from local employer demand — typically digital marketing, graphic design, video editing, web development, digital accounting, Python programming and data analysis. CodersTrust has run programs since 2014 in 15 countries and territories.

### Can It Reach Women and Marginalized Groups?

For women, yes. For other priority groups, we have early delivery experience and measure outcomes in each pilot. Women-focused programs report their own results: WSDFM 71% in work; Women in Online Work, Kosovo 68%; Her Power 372 of 800 tracked women in work (47%), tracked from the 2,500 trained directly by CodersTrust. Our first UNDP-supported pilot (2015) trained youth in Dhaka’s Korail settlement, and we are planning a program with the Bangladesh Computer Council and Bandhu Welfare Society to train 5,000 third-gender people. YouthWIDE in Bangladesh aims to reach madrasah youth, climate-vulnerable coastal youth and indigenous communities, with women a priority in every group. The randomized trial studied women aged 18–35 in Dhaka with at least a Secondary School Certificate (SSC), so it does not speak to the other groups.

### What About Safeguarding and Data Protection?

Safeguarding and data protection are specified for each program and agreed before enrollment. Each program design covers accessible selection, consent-based data collection, protected records, grievance handling, harassment and fraud response and referral pathways, with guardian consent and supervision for any participant under 18; funders receive privacy-appropriate data.

### How Do You Prove Outcomes?

For every cohort, as agreed in the program scope, we plan a baseline at enrollment, tracer surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months and monthly reports. Documentary evidence of earnings is collected where available: offer letters document job offers; payslips document employment earnings; platform logs or bank receipts document freelance earnings. Monthly reports cover enrollment, completion, certification, placements and first earnings. Independent evaluation is welcome.

### Is There Independent Evidence?

Yes — a randomized controlled trial by BIGD (BRAC University) of CodersTrust’s WSDFM program studied women in Dhaka trained in 2020, on a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track. It found that women offered a place were more likely to be employed than the control group. The trial surveyed 599 women at follow-up (report published May 2022). For context, a review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

### What Does It Cost?

Pricing is set per program, and we share an indicative budget in the discovery session. Standard inclusions are outreach and selection, blended training, an AI-ready curriculum, certification exam preparation (exam fee included), three months of mentoring, placement support, monthly reporting and outcome tracking for 12 months after each course; devices, stipends, connectivity and independent evaluation are budgeted separately where a program needs them. AI-ready curriculum: each track’s AI content and work-entry plan are agreed with you before enrollment; we do not yet publish a track-by-track breakdown.

### Will AI Eliminate the Jobs CodersTrust Trains For?

Some entry-level tasks in our tracks are vulnerable to automation, so we choose tracks from current employer demand, set per country. Within eight months of ChatGPT’s release, postings for automation-prone writing and coding work on a major freelancing platform fell 21% relative to manual-intensive work, while the World Economic Forum expects AI and information processing to create 11 million jobs and displace 9 million worldwide by 2030. AI-ready curriculum: each track’s AI content and work-entry plan are agreed with you before enrollment; we do not yet publish a track-by-track breakdown.

### What Happens When Funding Ends?

Where included in the program scope, what the program builds stays in place: certified local facilitators and localized curricula, and completers join CodersTrust’s alumni network after their three months of mentoring. TalentLEAP is designed to organize skills into career paths with progressive levels, so completers can keep advancing after a funded cohort ends.

### Can You Deliver at National Scale?

We scale in phases, starting from what we have done. Her Power, a national program of DoICT, the ICT Division, trained 25,125 women across 130 sub-districts (upazilas). CodersTrust provided the curriculum, training manuals and mentorship guidelines and trained 2,500 women directly. Six government contracts also have training scopes ranging from 120+ professionals to 3,120+ digital-lab staff. Separately, 10,000 teachers were trained to teach online during COVID-19. Larger programs run hub-and-spoke: up to 10 cohorts in parallel, with certified local facilitators and blended delivery in existing labs and colleges. In Bangladesh, 9,000+ government digital labs could host blended learning; lab access and seats are confirmed per program. A 100,000+ national program is a design option we plan phase by phase; we have not yet delivered one at that size.
