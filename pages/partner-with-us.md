## What Is CodersTrust?

CodersTrust is a workforce-development organization that trains and mentors educated, unemployed and underemployed youth in emerging markets for digital work and connects them to local, remote and global work.

## From First Conversation to First Cohort

Four steps, then monthly reports. Who does what is clear from the start.
- ### Step 1: Discovery · Week 1

A conversation about your goals, priority groups and timeline.
- ### Step 2: Co-Design · Week 2

We shape tracks, cohorts, safeguarding and reporting with you; each track’s work-entry plan is approved before enrollment.
- ### Step 3: Agreement · Week 3

Scope, schedule and the pilot scorecard are agreed.
- ### Step 4: First Cohort · 6–8 Weeks

Grant-funded pilots: training starts in this window after signing. Monthly reports follow.

## Partner FAQs

The 14 questions partners ask before they start, answered in full.

### Will This Work in Our Country?

CodersTrust has run programs since 2014 in 15 countries and territories; our outcome evidence comes mainly from Bangladesh, including a randomized trial of a 200-hour predecessor program; the Kosovo pilot adds program results. Each country version is localized with local partners, tracks and certifications. The course menu is set from local employer demand — typically digital marketing, graphic design, video editing, web development, digital accounting, Python programming and data analysis.

### Can You Deliver at National Scale?

We scale in phases, starting from what we have done: government contracts with training scopes ranging from 120+ professionals to 3,120+ digital-lab staff, and a national program that trained 25,125 women across 130 upazilas, for which CodersTrust provided the curriculum, training manuals and mentorship guidelines and trained 2,500 women directly; separately, 10,000 teachers were trained to teach online during COVID-19. Larger programs run hub-and-spoke — up to 10 cohorts in parallel, certified local facilitators and blended delivery in existing labs and colleges; in Bangladesh, 9,000+ government digital labs could host blended learning; lab access and seats are confirmed per program. A 100,000+ national program is a design option we plan phase by phase; we have not yet delivered one at that size.

### How Do You Prove Outcomes?

Baseline at enrollment, tracer surveys at 3, 6 and 12 months, and documentary evidence of earnings where available; independent evaluation is welcome. Offer letters document job offers; payslips document employment earnings; platform logs or bank receipts document freelance earnings where available. Funders receive monthly reports on enrollment, completion, certification, placements and first earnings.

### Is There Independent Evidence?

Yes — a randomized controlled trial by BIGD (BRAC University) of CodersTrust’s WSDFM program studied women in Dhaka trained in 2020, on a 200-hour predecessor of today’s 100-hour track. It found that women offered a place were more likely to be employed than the control group. The trial surveyed 599 women at follow-up (report published May 2022). For context, a review of nine randomized trials of vocational training by World Bank economist David McKenzie (2017) found only three with significant employment impacts.

### Will AI Eliminate the Jobs CodersTrust Trains For?

Some entry-level tasks in our tracks are exposed, so we choose tracks from current employer demand, set per country. Within eight months of ChatGPT’s release, postings for automation-prone writing and coding work on a major freelancing platform fell 21% relative to manual-intensive work, while the World Economic Forum expects AI and information processing to create 11 million jobs and displace 9 million worldwide by 2030. AI-ready curriculum: each track’s AI content and work-entry plan are agreed with you before enrollment; we do not yet publish a track-by-track breakdown.

### Can It Reach Women and Marginalized Groups?

For women, yes; for the most excluded groups we have early experience, and we measure outcomes in each pilot. Women-focused programs report their own results: WSDFM 71% in work; Women in Online Work, Kosovo 68%; Her Power 47% of its training cohort. Our first UNDP-supported pilot (2015) trained youth in Dhaka’s Korail settlement, and we are planning a program with the Bangladesh Computer Council and Bandhu Welfare Society to train 5,000 third-gender people. YouthWIDE in Bangladesh aims to reach madrasah youth, climate-vulnerable coastal youth and indigenous communities, with women a priority in every group. The randomized trial studied women aged 18–35 in Dhaka with at least a Secondary School Certificate (SSC), so it does not speak to the other groups.

### How Fast Can We Start?

For grant-funded pilots, about three weeks from first conversation to agreement, with the first cohort in training 6–8 weeks after signing. Week 1 is a discovery session, week 2 co-design and week 3 the agreement; public procurement follows the agency’s own timeline.

### What Does It Cost?

Pricing is set per program, and we share an indicative budget in the discovery session. Standard inclusions are outreach and selection, blended training, an AI-ready curriculum, certification exam preparation (exam fee included), three months of mentoring, placement support, monthly reporting and outcome tracking for 12 months after each course; devices, stipends, connectivity and independent evaluation are budgeted separately where a program needs them. AI-ready curriculum: each track’s AI content and work-entry plan are agreed with you before enrollment; we do not yet publish a track-by-track breakdown.

### What About Safeguarding and Data Protection?

Safeguarding and data protection are specified for each program and agreed before enrollment. Each program design covers accessible selection, consent-based data collection, protected records, grievance handling, harassment and fraud response and referral pathways, with guardian consent and supervision for any participant under 18; funders receive privacy-appropriate data.

### What Happens When Funding Ends?

Where included in the program scope, what the program builds stays in place: certified local facilitators and localized curricula, and completers join CodersTrust’s alumni network after their three months of mentoring. TalentLEAP is designed to organize skills into career paths with progressive levels, so completers can keep advancing after a funded cohort ends.

### Is the JobReady Platform Live?

Partly — modules are rolling out in stages. Live today: partner portals, marketing automation, CRM and AI chat. In pre-launch: the employer and gig-work (Earn) marketplace, so placement also runs through CodersTrust’s placement team and marketplaces such as Upwork and Fiverr.

### Do Your Learners Only Freelance?

No — salaried jobs remain the largest pathway; freelancing and business ownership grew the most in relative terms. Among surveyed completers, salaried employment rose from 38.3% before training to 53.3% at the October 2026 survey, freelancing from 6.2% to 14.0% and business ownership from 2.8% to 10.6%.

### Who Are Your Typical Learners?

Mostly university graduates from across Bangladesh, some of them already working before training. Among surveyed completers, 84.6% hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, 68.7% were aged 15–35 and 62.9% live outside Dhaka, across 58 of the country’s 64 districts; 47.4% were working before training.

### How Is CodersTrust Different from an Online Course Platform?

Self-paced course platforms focus on content access; our programs add mentoring, certification exam preparation and placement support, agreed in each program’s scope. Every TalentLEAP cohort pairs 100 training hours per certification with three months of milestone-based mentoring, certification exam preparation (exam fee included), placement support and outcome tracking for 12 months.
