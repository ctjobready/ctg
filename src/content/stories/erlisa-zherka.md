Erlisa Zherka was a 22-year-old finance math student from Kosovo when she enrolled in the "Women in Online Work" pilot project. She received her online work training in the municipality of Lipjan and soon started making more than the country's wage average ($330-440, per the Kosovo 2015 Labor Market Survey). Her duties included testing proprietary software and customer relations.

She recently landed a full-time online contract with a US-based car brokerage startup through the Women in Online Work (WoW) program, a pilot that aims at rapidly training up to 100 beneficiaries on technical (IT) and soft skills that are in demand by the ever-growing online freelancing market.

If asked before her enrollment in the pilot, Erlisa would not have believed this type of opportunity would be possible. "Everyone was surprised to learn this exists," she explained when describing the reaction of her family and friends.
